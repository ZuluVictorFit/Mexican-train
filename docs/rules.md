# Rule decisions and proposed first preset

Mexican Train has household variations. This is a product decision sheet, not a
claim that one universal rulebook exists. External sources have not yet been
retrieved; see [research status](research.md). User choices take precedence.

## Current play style (November 4 update)

The group wants a less guided game. Players may draw once on their turn even
when they overlook a playable tile, and may then play any legal tile in their
hand or end their turn and open their train. With an empty boneyard they may
pass; a round is blocked only when nobody actually has a legal play. The game
still rejects invalid placements and maintains double obligations. The interface
no longer highlights playable trains or identifies an available move. A delayed,
general reminder suggests drawing when a player cannot play. An optional guided
mode may be added later. These decisions supersede earlier mandatory-draw and
forced-play wording below where it conflicts.

## Confirmed rules

- At most six players and exactly one community train.
- After opening turns, any player may start the community train on their turn,
  even if they have not started their personal train.
- When a player plays a tile on their own open train, remove its marker; their
  train becomes private again.
- Playing on the community train or another player's train does not close the
  player's own open train.
- If the boneyard is empty and a player has no legal play, they mark their own
  train open and pass.
- An exposed double must be covered before play on another train. If the player
  cannot cover it, the obligation passes to the next player(s).
- A player may draw one tile per turn even if they have a play. After drawing,
  they may play any legal tile or end their turn and open their personal train.
- On a player's first turn, they may lay as many matching tiles as desired on
  their personal train.
- A player's first turn is for their own train, except that an exposed double
  must be covered first. They may draw one and then play or end their turn.
  Players still taking their first turn cannot play on another open train as a
  normal move; the exposed-double obligation takes priority.
- After playing their opening tiles, a player may end the opening turn.
- If that drawn tile starts the player's personal train, they may continue their
  opening run with additional matching tiles.
- Deal 15 tiles each for 2–4 players and 12 tiles each for 5–6 players, using a
  double-12 set. Set the round's starting double aside before dealing, then place
  it in the center.
- After covering a double in an opening run, the player may continue adding
  matching tiles to their personal train.
- Outside the opening turn, a player who covers their own double ends their turn.
- Covering another player's exposed double ends the turn, even if it is the
  covering player's first turn. If a double prevents someone from starting their
  train on their first turn, their later turns use the normal one-tile rule;
  they do not get a delayed opening run.
- When a player's last tile is a double, they immediately draw one and try to
  cover it. If they cannot, they keep the drawn tile and the normal cover rule
  applies; play continues. The double must be covered before the round can end.
- If the boneyard is already empty when the last tile is played, the other players
  try to cover the double; once it is covered, the player who emptied their hand
  goes out. If nobody can cover it, the round is blocked and everyone scores
  the tiles left in their hand.
- Host chooses a full 12-to-0 game or a shorter starting double.
- Score remaining tiles by the sum of their numbers; 0–0 is zero. Lowest total wins.
- If players tie for the lowest final game total, the tied player with the lowest
  score in the final round wins. If still tied, select randomly among those still
  tied to produce one winner.
- Choose the first round's starting player at random. The previous round's winner
  starts the next round. In a blocked round, the player with the lowest score
  for that round is the winner; if players tie for lowest, choose the next starter
  randomly among the tied players.

## Double example

Morgan plays 6–6 on an open route. That endpoint now requires a 6. Morgan may
cover with a matching tile; if none, draw one. If Morgan ends the turn without covering it, Morgan opens their own train and
the next player must cover it before touching any other route. The community train does not bypass the obligation.

The exact marker behavior while a double is unresolved remains to be confirmed.
After covering a double in a first-turn run, the player may continue building
their personal train. Also decide whether a
player can voluntarily end an opening without laying a tile. On a first turn,
players must attempt to cover an exposed double before starting their own train.
Covering another player's double ends the turn, and an opening opportunity lost
to a double does not carry forward to a later turn.
The opening-run UI needs
an explicit End Opening Turn action; do not auto-end after the first nondouble.
Do not bury these cases in rendering or infer them from an attractive mockup.

An exposed double is covered by playing a matching tile on that same route. With
one standard double-12 set, the only matching double is already on the table, so
another double cannot directly cover it. A later double in an opening run can be
played after that obligation is covered and creates a new obligation of its own.
A blocked-round check must count consecutive completed
turns without a play, reset on any play, and apply only when the boneyard is empty;
the obligation also restricts which plays are legal. When a final double cannot
be covered by any player with an empty boneyard, the confirmed result is a blocked
round: score each hand, including the empty hand, without claiming a cover occurred.

## Worked examples for future tests

These examples use the confirmed house rules; they do not replace the full rule
matrix or decide edge cases still marked open.

1. **Deal:** At a six-player table, set the round's engine aside, then deal 12
   tiles to each player. The 90 non-engine tiles consist of 72 dealt and 18 in
   the boneyard.
2. **Opening draw:** A player cannot start their train, draws a matching tile,
   and plays it. They may continue their opening run using other matching tiles;
   once done they end the turn without drawing to extend it further.
3. **Last tile double, draw fails:** A player's last tile is 6–6. They immediately
   draw 2–3, which cannot cover the 6–6, and keep it. Their train opens and the
   next player must attempt the cover; the game continues.
4. **Last tile double, no tiles to draw:** A player lays their last tile, 6–6,
   with an empty boneyard. Other players attempt the cover. If someone covers it,
   the original player goes out. If nobody can, the round is blocked and everyone
   scores their remaining tiles, including zero for the empty hand.
5. **Final-game tie:** After the last round, two players are tied for the lowest
   cumulative score. If their final-round scores are 4 and 3, respectively,
   the player with 3 wins. If their final-round scores also tie, randomly choose
   the winner among the players still tied.

## Host rule options

Start with one named **House rules v1** preset after the outstanding decisions.
Show the full summary in the lobby. A shorter starting engine is the first
recommended option. Only add additional presets that we can name, explain, and
test; do not expose a matrix of incompatible toggles at launch.

Later candidate variations: single-tile openings, different initial hand sizes,
blank-double penalties, engine-in-deal starts, and alternate double handling.
Unsupported combinations must be rejected before a game starts. Configuration
is immutable during a game and stored with its version in every saved room.

## Required rule-engine validation after implementation

- Exactly 91 unique tiles, immutable face identities, and conservation of tiles
  across engine, hands, boneyard, and routes.
- Correct deal and scoring for each supported player count and engine schedule.
- A final-total tie is resolved using only the tied players' final-round scores;
  if those are also tied, random selection includes only the players still tied.
- Legal/illegal personal, open-opponent, and community moves; engine matching.
- Exactly one community train, including simultaneous/stale start attempts.
- Double covering, inability to cover, obligation passing, and end behavior for a
  last-tile double, including a matching draw, a nonmatching draw, an empty
  boneyard followed by a cover, and nobody able to cover.
- Draw/pass restrictions, playable draw, empty boneyard, closing markers, blocked
  rounds, score accumulation, and next-round starting player.
- Stale, duplicate, non-active-player, and fabricated-tile actions cannot mutate
  the state. Accepted actions always produce a deterministic next state.
